## Introduction to Logs

Attackers try to minimize the traces they leave behind to avoid detection. Despite this, security teams are often able to reconstruct how an attack occurred and sometimes even identify the attacker. This is possible because **traces are almost always left behind**, even in digital environments.

Just as physical investigations rely on clues like footprints, damaged doors, or CCTV footage, digital investigations rely on **logs**. Logs act as **digital footprints**, recording activities that occur within systems. These activities may be normal or malicious. By analyzing logs, security teams can trace actions back to their source and understand what happened.
## What Are Logs?

Logs are records generated by systems, applications, and devices that capture events and activities. They store valuable traces of user actions, system behavior, errors, and potential attacks. Logs are one of the most important sources of evidence during security investigations.
## Use Cases of Logs

### Security Events Monitoring

Logs help detect abnormal or suspicious behavior through continuous or real-time monitoring.

### Incident Investigation and Forensics

Logs provide detailed records of activities before, during, and after an incident. They are essential for root cause analysis and forensic investigations.

### Troubleshooting

Since logs record errors and system issues, they are useful for diagnosing and resolving application or system problems.

### Performance Monitoring

Logs offer insights into system and application performance, helping identify bottlenecks or inefficiencies.

### Auditing and Compliance

Logs create an activity trail that supports auditing requirements and regulatory compliance.
